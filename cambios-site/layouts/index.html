CambiOS is a desktop operating system we're building from scratch, meant for the machine in front of you — the one that runs Windows or macOS today. Your identity is a key you create, and no one else manages. Every file and message is indelibly stamped with who — or what — made it. CambiOS never phones home, because it is home.

No shipping general-purpose OS holds all five of the following together. Each was built for a different problem and made different tradeoffs. CambiOS is the bet that the next era needs all five, and that retrofitting won't get there.

  • Architecturally enforced security. A five-job microkernel, everything else in user space, a capability check at every boundary.
  • Sovereign identity. Your identity is a key the kernel enforces. No vendor account between you and your machine, no password to phish.
  • Sovereign data. Content-addressed storage owned by whoever made it. No service in the middle scanning, indexing, or reporting.
  • Sovereign network. An identity-routed overlay where the destination is a public key, so infrastructure can't surveil or redirect what it can't decrypt.
  • AI as infrastructure, optional by tier. Local models that verify, watch, and translate — and a kernel that runs without any of it.

How each of these compares to what exists is in why not just use X?

Provenance

Generative AI has broken "seeing is believing." We can't tell you what's real. No one can, from the pixels alone. What CambiOS inherently tells you, unforgeably, is who or what made it: every message and file is stamped, in the kernel, with the identity of its author — a person, a device, or an AI agent, each a principal. The stamp can't be forged, and it can't be peeled off without breaking the object.

Knowing who claims authorship is a part of this problem the machine can settle. The decision to trust them stays yours — as it always has. Reputations are built between people, over time, and a name you can't fake is what lets one mean something. That web of who-vouches-for-whom is a userspace concern, peer to peer, with no authority in the middle — which is exactly where the kernel's refusal to judge identity leaves it.

Social networking

The point of a name you can't fake is the people on the other end of it. Your identity starts as a key only you hold. It gains weight through the people who know you: when two people who have actually met vouch for each other, each signs a record of that relationship, and both keep it. Enough of those, overlapping, and who you are is anchored by who knows you rather than by a platform's records — a web that is hard to counterfeit, because every strand in it was signed by someone already inside it.

Communities form the same way: a group is the people whose vouching connects, and membership is something you can prove, not something a server grants. No platform sits in the middle to rank these relationships, rent them back to you, or revoke them. The decentralized-identity world is converging on the same shapes — verifiable relationship credentials, trust graphs, verifiable trust communities — and CambiOS is built to speak to that work without being owned by any one ecosystem (Identity Synthesis). This layer is designed, not yet built; the identity and provenance beneath it run today.

Where we are today

This is working code, not a design document. Two x86_64 machines boot it from a USB stick to the shell; a 12-core Dell Precision 3630 comes all the way up to a GUI terminal with a USB keyboard. It is not yet a desktop you can live in. Networking and NVMe storage on hardware are next, then the application layer. Status tracks every subsystem, every test count, and every known gap.

Runs on real hardware
Microkernel in Rust, every driver and service an isolated user-space process, started by a signed boot manifest. x86_64 on bare metal; AArch64 and RISC-V at full service parity under QEMU.
Identity and provenance
Every process gets an Ed25519 identity, bound by the kernel at spawn. Every message and object carries its author's unforgeable stamp. A user-space vault signs per Principal; smart-card keys ride the USB pipe.
Storage and encryption
Content-addressed, signed objects that survive reboot, over full-disk encryption unlocked through the vault. A POSIX file model is taking shape alongside.
Verified where it counts
48 Kani proof harnesses across 6 crates back the allocators, ELF parser, capability manager, user-slice validators, and DTB parser. Writing them fixed 10 real overflow bugs. CLAIMS.md tracks proven versus aspirational.

All of this lives in software. The privileged silicon underneath — Intel ME, AMD PSP, Apple's Secure Enclave, the TPM stack — is a separate fight, and not one CambiOS pretends to win. For now, the software side has to stop adding to the problem. Change starts with awareness and we'll cross that bridge when we get there.

Commitments

No telemetry. No analytics, no phone-home, no usage reporting. Telemetry was never part of the design -- there's nothing to turn off.

No backdoors. No remote access mechanism that bypasses the capability model. No "emergency" channels. No "debug" overrides that work without the user's explicit action.

Your stack is yours. No DRM. No activation. No mandatory accounts. Secure boot protects your boot chain from tampering -- the signing keys are yours.
